### Modeling Capabilities

This document summarizes all modeling components in Owl (Optimal wealth lab), how each is implemented, and the assumptions and limitations that apply. It serves as a quick reference for understanding the scope and boundaries of the optimizer.

| Component | Modeling approach | Assumptions & limitations |
|------------|--------------------|---------------------------|
| **Objectives** | maxSpending: maximize net spending subject to bequest floor. maxBequest: maximize after-tax bequest subject to spending floor; requires `netSpending` option to set the spending floor. Roth conversion amounts optimized in both (subject to caps and per-person controls). Optional `timePreference` (%/year) discounts future spending to reduce end-of-life back-loading. To see the trade-off between the two objectives rather than one point on it, sweep the `bequest` floor under maxSpending to trace the spending-vs-bequest trade-off curve. | LP objective is linear, so it always drives spending to an extreme of the feasible range; use profile slack and bequest/spending constraints to shape the solution. |
| **Spending profile** | User-specified shape (flat or smile) scaling a spending basis, the profile-neutral level: the profile averages to it over the plan, and first-year spending is the basis times the profile's first value. Survivorship factor at first death. Optimizer determines achievable basis. The profile is a bilateral constraint for both objectives: spending stays within ±slack% of the profile shape (`spendingSlack` sets the tolerance). | Profile shape fixed; only scale is optimized. |
| **Bequest objective** | After-tax estate value. Tax-deferred and HSA balances discounted by heirs' marginal rate. Surviving spouse inherits per beneficiary fractions; non-spouse portion taxed. | Single heirs' marginal tax rate applied to all inherited tax-deferred and HSA balances. |
| **Taxable accounts** | Dividend and interest yield proportional to balance each year; capital gains when optimizer elects to sell. All realized gains treated as long-term. Capital gains on withdrawals use the **average-cost** method: when a fraction of the account is sold, the same fraction of the total cost basis is recovered, so the realized gain equals `gain_fraction × withdrawal` where `gain_fraction = (balance − basis) / balance`. Optional: `setCostBasis([b1, b2])` (or TOML field `taxable_cost_basis`) supplies the current cost basis per person; the SC loop then tracks how basis evolves each year as shares are sold and new contributions (HFP contributions plus LP surplus deposits) add fresh basis, as do the dividends and interest taxed each year and reinvested in the account. Bond and cash returns are taxed yearly, so the account's unrealized gain is attributed to its equity share. Without a supplied basis, realized gains fall back to the previous year's price appreciation only: the equity share of the withdrawal times the prior year's S&P 500 return less the dividend rate, floored at zero, which underestimates gains for accounts with substantial embedded appreciation. | All gains treated as long-term; short-term gains (holding period &lt; 1 year) not distinguished. Average-cost method only; FIFO and LIFO lot accounting not supported. Tax-loss harvesting not modeled. |
| **Start date (first year)** | Each plan year runs from January 1, and the first year is a full calendar year. Balances entered as of a later date are back-projected to January 1 for growth only, at the first year's assumed returns. Graphs and worksheets show the whole first year; the summary shows the net spending remaining in the year (first-year spending times the fraction of the year left). | Money already moved through the accounts this year is not added back, so with a start after January 1 the first year counts it again: spending paid so far (net of income received) is charged again, contributions already made are added again. Use January 1 balances when available; otherwise enter only the contributions, QCDs and big-ticket items still to come in the first year of the Wages and Contributions table, keeping wages (like SS and pensions) at full-year amounts, since that income offsets the spending already paid. Spending, an annual target, cannot be split this way; to offset it, add to the balances (most naturally the taxable account) the spending and taxes paid since January 1 minus the wages, benefits and pensions received since then. That net is positive for a retiree drawing down savings and usually negative for someone still saving. |
| **Safety net (taxable)** | Optional inflation-indexed minimum balance per individual's taxable account. Enforced from year 2 through each individual's life horizon. | First year excluded. Can cause infeasibility if minimum exceeds initial balance or desired bequest is smaller than survivor's safety net. |
| **Tax-deferred accounts** (401k, IRA, 403b) | Pre-tax contributions from Wages and Contributions table. Withdrawals taxed as ordinary income. RMDs enforced as minimum withdrawal floors from age 70–75 (birth-year dependent). 10% early withdrawal penalty before age 59½. | Pro-rata rule (IRS Form 8606) not modeled; entire balance treated as pre-tax. Early withdrawal exceptions (72(t), disability, etc.) not modeled. |
| **Roth accounts** | After-tax contributions. Five-year maturation rule enforced via minimum-balance constraints (recent conversions + contribution gains retained): a conversion is held through its five-taxable-year period, the year of the conversion and the four after it (IRC 408A(d)(3)(F)), and before 59½ only conversions past it count as withdrawable basis, so a same-year conversion and withdrawal cannot take earnings out early. Age 59½ threshold per individual. Conversion amounts optimized; taxable as ordinary income in year of conversion. Individual years can be held fixed instead of optimized by ticking the *Roth conv fixed* column of the Wages & Contributions table beside the amount in *Roth conv*: the conversion is then pinned to exactly that amount, bypassing the annual cap and the last-two-years restriction, and an amount of `0` pins the year at no conversion. | Contribution principal not separately tracked; some valid early withdrawals of principal conservatively disallowed. Pro-rata rule not modeled. Roth conversions disallowed in last two years of plan horizon (unless that year is held fixed). |
| **Health Savings Accounts (HSA)** | Fourth account type. Contributions are deducted through the wages they are netted from, which keeps them out of ordinary income, provisional income, and MAGI; without wages, a negative `other inc` entered alongside carries the above-the-line deduction (IRC §223(a), §62(a)(19)) and pays the contribution from cash. Contributions stop at Medicare enrollment (IRC §223(b)(7)): the year it starts keeps the months before it. Withdrawals capped at total qualified medical expenses (QMEs): Medicare costs plus `setMedicalExpenses()` amount (dental, vision, co-pays, etc.), which grows with inflation plus any `setMedicalInflation()` excess. Pre-Medicare years: only the `setMedicalExpenses()` amount is eligible. Spouse inherits intact. | IRS contribution limits not enforced; user responsible. HDHP eligibility assumed, not verified. Catch-up (\$1,000 for 55+) not auto-applied. A deduction larger than the year's income is lost (no carryover). Pre-65 non-qualified penalty (20%) not modeled for withdrawals exceeding QMEs. |
| **Fixed assets** | Real estate, annuities, collectibles, etc. An asset is listed from the first year it is present in January, so one bought during 2026 carries `year` 2027, and one sold during its `yod` is still listed that year. The proceeds are cash flow of that year: taxed then, and available for that year's spending and a linked loan; any surplus is deposited that year and first shows in the next January's balance. Disposition generates proceeds split into ordinary income, LTCG, and tax-free portions by asset type. Growth rate from the reference year to the start of the disposition year: real (above inflation) for `residence`, `real estate`, `collectibles` and `precious metals`, nominal for `stocks` and `fixed annuity`. Sales commission applied. **Type-specific tax treatment:** `residence` — IRC §121 primary residence exclusion applied (\$250k single / \$500k MFJ; a surviving spouse keeps \$500k for a sale within two years of the death, IRC §121(b)(4)); gain above the exclusion is LTCG, excluded gain and basis are tax-free. `fixed annuity` — gain above basis is ordinary income; basis returned tax-free. All other types (`real estate`, `stocks`, `collectibles`, `precious metals`) — full gain is LTCG, basis is tax-free. | Fixed outflows; disposition timing and amount user-specified. No optimization of sale timing. |
| **Debts** | Amortized payments from start year for term years. Principal and interest from standard amortization. Enters cash-flow as fixed outflows. A loan linked to a property (`property`) is paid off in the year that property is sold. | No optimization of paydown vs. invest. Early payoff only through the sale of a linked residence or real estate; a loan cannot be paid down from savings in a chosen year. Mortgage interest not deducted (standard deduction assumed). |
| **Wages & contributions** | Year-indexed table: wages, other income, net inv, contributions to all account types, Roth conversions, QCDs, big-ticket items. Five-year lookback for Roth maturation. Only the `year` column is required; any other column may be omitted and is read as zero. Wages are the wages in AGI (pay minus pre-tax 401k, deductible IRA and payroll HSA contributions); contributions are deposited without leaving the cash flow, so an after-tax one paid from pay (Roth, taxable) is paid for by a negative big-ticket item of the same amount (#221). | User-supplied; no optimization of contribution amounts. HSA contributions stop at Medicare enrollment, prorated by month in the year it starts. Only `other inc` (a deduction) and `big-ticket items` (an expense) may be negative. |
| **Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs)** | Per-person, per-year amounts from the `QCD` column of the Wages & Contributions table. Excluded from AGI, so they lower federal and state taxable income, Social Security taxability, and the MAGI feeding IRMAA and NIIT. Credited against the RMD dollar-for-dollar, drawn from the tax-deferred balance, and never available as spending. Age 70.5 and the annual per-person limit (\$108k in 2025, \$115k in 2026, indexed; later years projected at a fixed 3%) are enforced as errors. Reported as a `Charitable giving` slice in the lifetime-allocation and cash-flow-mix charts, as a dedicated *Charitable Giving (QCD)* chart splitting each gift at the RMD it satisfies, as its own worksheet column, and in the case description. | Amount is user-specified, not optimized. IRA-only eligibility not checked: Owl aggregates IRA with 401(k)/403(b) into one tax-deferred account. A 401(k) balance must be rolled over to an IRA first to be eligible, which is a real-world step but a non-event in the model. Anti-abuse offset for deductible IRA contributions after 70.5 not modeled. One-time split-interest-entity QCD (CGA/CRT) not modeled. |
| **Social Security benefits** | PIA × age-based adjustment factor. FRA by birth year (65–67). Own, spousal, and survivor benefits computed from PIA and DOB. Benefits are prorated in the year they start, by the claiming month (same as pension). Taxability (0–85%) from provisional income formula converged via the SC loop. Optional trim for trust-fund scenarios. Claiming ages are user-specified by default. Optional: `withSSTaxability="optimize"` replaces the SC-loop taxability approximation with an exact MILP formulation. Optional: `withSSAges="optimize"` (or one individual's name) chooses SS claiming ages (monthly resolution, 62–70, never a month already past) by solving the plan at each open month and keeping the best; with both spouses selected, each age is searched in turn with the other held. Scenario runs keep the plan's claiming ages. The SSA earnings test is applied: from the first month of entitlement through the FRA year, benefits are withheld against the year's excess earnings (`anticipated wages` plus the year's `401k ctrb`, and `IRA ctrb` in a year with pay, which those wages are net of; `employer ctrb` left out; spread over `months worked`), and the months withheld raise the benefit from FRA through the adjustment of the reduction factor; under `withSSAges` every candidate claiming age is solved with it; where it withholds nothing the streams are the ones Owl builds without it, so a case with no earnings up to anyone's FRA year is unchanged. Reported as *SS withheld by the earnings test* and *ARF months* in the Summary (for every plan, \$0 and "none" included), a *SS withheld* column in the Sources worksheets, `ss_withheld_today` / `ss_withheld_nominal` in the plan metrics, the `earnings_test` block of `owlcli explain`, and a warning where a claiming age pays nothing before FRA. **Survivor benefits:** the survivor's own benefit and the survivor benefit are tracked as separate streams and the larger is paid each year, so a survivor who has not yet claimed keeps their own (still growing) benefit. Amount is set by the deceased's record — their PIA plus the DRCs earned up to death, fixed at death (no posthumous DRCs). The reduction is measured against the survivor's own age at filing, linearly from 71.5% at 60 to 100% at the survivor FRA (a distinct schedule from the retirement FRA, up to 4 months earlier); no credit accrues past it. If the deceased claimed before their FRA, the reduced amount is then capped at the greater of the deceased's reduced benefit and 82.5% of their PIA (Social Security Act §202(e)(2)(D)); the cap is not itself reduced. `social_security_survivor_claim_age` sets the timing: `"immediate"` (default), `"fra"`, or an explicit age. | Family maximum benefit cap not modeled. Earnings test: not applied to checks paid from the year of the first death on (survivor benefits under the test and the survivor adjustment of the reduction factor not modeled); exempt amounts as published through 2027, each in its year's dollars, and held at the 2027 level in real terms after (a plan starting after 2027 takes 2027's amounts as its own dollars, with a warning); earnings taken as spread evenly over the months worked; years before the plan taken to have no earnings. Survivor claiming date is user-specified, not optimized. With both spouses selected, the alternating search can stop on a pair that a joint search would improve. Claiming ages are chosen under the life expectancies entered, not over scenarios. WEP and GPO repealed (2025); not modeled. |
| **Pension income** | Monthly amount and commencement age per individual. Optional inflation indexing. First-year proration by fraction of year remaining. Joint-and-survivor fraction for survivor benefit. | Early-commencement reductions and late-commencement credits not modeled. |
| **SPIA (Single Premium Immediate Annuity)** | IRA/401k rollover funds a SPIA: premium deducted from tax-deferred account in buy year (non-taxable event); annual payments begin in the same year as purchase as fully taxable ordinary income. Optional CPI indexing. Optional joint-and-survivor fraction for couples. Payments increase MAGI, affecting Medicare IRMAA surcharges and Social Security taxability. Multiple SPIAs supported per plan. | Premium assumed drawn entirely from tax-deferred account (IRA rollover); non-IRA funding not modeled. Partial-year proration of income in buy year not modeled (full annual payment assumed). Annuity pricing (premium-to-income ratio) is user-supplied; no built-in pricing model. |
| **Ordinary income tax** | Seven brackets (OBBBA 2026: 10–37%). Standard deduction + 65+ additional + OBBBA §1002 bonus (phases out by MAGI). Bracket widths and rates inflation-adjusted. Optional reversion to pre-TCJA rates. | Itemized deductions not modeled; standard deduction only. OBBBA 65+ bonus phaseout follows the previous iteration's MAGI by default; `withSeniorBonus="optimize"` (or a `breakpointMethod` preset) solves it with the plan, with one binary per bonus year. A state that conforms to the federal deduction follows the previous iteration in either mode. |
| **State income tax** | All 50 states + DC supported via `state` field in `[basic_info]`. One change of state during the plan via `moves`: the state of residence on December 31 taxes the whole year, so the year of the move is taxed by the new state; the optimizer times conversions and withdrawals around it. Graduated brackets with per-year inflation adjustment. Filing status transitions MFJ→Single at spouse death. State standard deduction. Optional retirement income exemption (dollar cap, optionally age-gated, e.g. NY \$20k from 59½, CO \$24k from 65, KY \$31,110; IL fully exempt). Optional pension-only exemption (`pension_exemption`; no state uses it at present). Roth conversions count toward the retirement income exemption, with its age, except in MD (never exempt) and in PA and MS (left out of state income at any age, while their exemption of IRA withdrawals starts at 59½). Optional SS exclusion, a yes/no flag per state (most states, KS and MO included, exempt SS). Capital gains taxed as ordinary income at state level. All state LP variables are continuous (pure LP, no new binary variables). | State AMT, local taxes, state-specific credits not modeled. At most one move; part-year residency (income split between the two states in the year of the move) not modeled. AGI thresholds on the SS exclusion are not applied: the \$75k (KS) and \$100k (MO) figures in `taxes_state.toml` are unused, and SS is exempt in those states at any income. Retirement income exemption applies to IRA withdrawals only; SPIA and annuity income treated as ordinary. Bracket rates in `taxes_state.toml` require annual review. |
| **Long-term capital gains tax** | Three brackets (0%, 15%, 20%) based on total taxable income. LTCG stacks on ordinary income. By default, bracket thresholds are determined via the SC loop (prior-year ordinary income fixes bracket bounds; optimizer fills lowest-rate brackets first). Optional: `withLTCG="optimize"` replaces the SC-loop approximation with a MILP formulation, solved by branch-and-bound or local search (`mipStrategy`), using binary variables (`zl`) to select the correct 0%/15%/20% bracket each year. | All realized gains treated as long-term; short-term gains not modeled. Line 25 of the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet, the lesser of the stacked tax and the regular tax on all taxable income (IRC §1(h)(1)), is not taken: the 0% breakpoint sits below the top of the 12% bracket (\$49,450 and \$50,400 single, \$98,900 and \$100,800 joint in 2026), so gains in that gap pay 15% where line 25 would charge 12%, overstating the tax by at most 3% of the gap (\$28.50 single, \$57 joint a year), and only when gains fall in it. Taking the minimum would need a binary per year (#206). |
| **Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)** | 3.8% on lesser of NII and MAGI excess over \$200k (single) / \$250k (MFJ). NII = dividends, interest, LTCG, rent/trust from `net inv` column. Converged via self-consistent loop. Optional: `withNIIT="optimize"` uses a MILP formulation with one binary variable per year (`zj`) selecting which term of the minimum bounds the tax; most effective when `withLTCG="optimize"` is also set. | Thresholds not indexed for inflation (per IRS). |
| **Payroll tax (FICA)** | The employee's share, charged on the earnings the earnings test reads (`anticipated wages` plus the year's `401k ctrb`, even when it took all of the year's pay, and `IRA ctrb` in a year with pay; `employer ctrb` is never earnings): social security tax 6.2% of each person's earnings up to the wage base (\$184,500 in 2026, \$192,300 in 2027), Medicare tax 1.45% of all earnings, and the Additional Medicare Tax, 0.9% of the household's combined earnings above \$250,000 through the year of the first death and \$200,000 after. Always on: a constant outflow of the year's cash-flow balance (no variable, constraint, or binary), not deductible, so it enters no income-tax computation and stays out of the effective tax rate. Reported as *Total payroll tax paid* in the Summary (\$0 without earnings), a *payroll tax* column in the Cash Flow and Taxes worksheets, `payroll_tax_today` / `payroll_tax_nominal` in the plan metrics, and on its own beside the income tax: a line in the taxes plot, and a *Payroll tax* slice and band in the lifetime allocation and the annual cash-flow mix. | Self-employment tax not modeled: all earnings are taken as wages, so net SE earnings entered in `anticipated wages` pay the employee's 7.65%, about half of SE tax, and the half of SE tax deducted from AGI is not deducted; `other inc` pays none. Wage base held at the 2027 level in real terms after 2027 (by statute it follows the national average wage index). The Additional Medicare Tax's thresholds are not indexed (per IRS). Employer contributions entered in `401k ctrb` rather than `employer ctrb` make the earnings high by 7.65% of them; pay that all went to a deductible IRA is not counted (it looks like a spousal IRA); a nondeductible IRA is not supported (no basis) and would be counted twice; a payroll HSA is left out (not FICA wages). |
| **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** | IRS Uniform Lifetime Table III for standard cases; IRS Joint and Last Survivor Table II (Pub 590-B) when spouse is the sole beneficiary and more than 10 years younger. Starting age: 70 (pre-1949), 72 (1949–1950), 73 (1951–1959), 75 (1960+). Fractions applied to prior-year-end balance. | Inherited IRA and beneficiary RMD rules not modeled. |
| **Medicare Part B & Part D** | Part B and Part D premiums (base + IRMAA surcharges) from MAGI two years prior, on the table of the filing status of that lookback return: a survivor's first two years after the first death still use the joint table. Part D uses same brackets as Part B; optional base premium (default 0) and optional disable for other drug coverage. Premiums (base and IRMAA surcharges) grow with inflation, compounded with an optional excess `setMedicalInflation()` (TOML `medical_inflation_excess`, %/year, default 0); IRMAA thresholds follow inflation alone. By default, IRMAA surcharges are determined via the SC loop (prior MAGI fixes bracket selection each iteration). Optional: `withMedicare="optimize"` replaces the SC-loop approximation with a MILP formulation for bracket selection; can be disabled to reduce MIP size and improve solve time when IRMAA precision is not needed. | IRMAA thresholds assumed to scale with general inflation; CMS may use different index. The top tier (\$500k single, \$750k MFJ) is held fixed through 2027 and indexed from 2028, as set by the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018. Medical price growth is a single constant excess over inflation, the same for every year and every scenario (not stochastic). First two plan years require user-supplied prior MAGI (default zero). Part D base is user-configurable; default 0. |
| **ACA marketplace (pre-65)** | Net Premium Tax Credit (PTC) cost: user sets SLCSP benchmark premium, which grows with inflation plus any `setMedicalInflation()` excess; PTC computed from MAGI and FPL (FPL follows inflation alone). For couples, the SLCSP should be the combined household premium; when the older spouse transitions to Medicare, the SLCSP is automatically scaled to the remaining spouse's individual plan using the CMS age rating curve (45 CFR 147.102), prorating by age-rating factors at the transition year. Optional start year defers ACA coverage (years before start treated as employer-covered, zero ACA cost). Two modes: **loop** (default, `withACA` unset or `"loop"`) uses SC iteration with year-aware acaCosts; **optimize** (`withACA="optimize"`) uses LP/MIP to co-optimize MAGI vs ACA cost. Medicaid follows the state of residence each year: at no premium up to 138% FPL in expansion states, 100% in WI; the other states without the expansion (AL, FL, GA, KS, MS, SC, TN, TX, WY) leave a coverage gap below 100% FPL, where there is no premium tax credit and the full SLCSP is owed. | **Loop**: Year-aware: 2025 rules, then each year's applicable percentage table from 2026 on (2027 included; later years carry the last one). Exact sliding scale. ACA fixed per MAGI each iteration. **Optimize**: Rules of 2026 on, with each year's applicable percentage table; a 2025 plan year is priced on them too. Sliding scale priced by tangent lines (a few dollars below the statute at most). LP can reduce ACA by shifting MAGI, including down to the Medicaid line, and prices the coverage gap exactly. **Loop** charges the lowest applicable percentage in the coverage gap (no jump to oscillate across) and warns when a year ends there, understating its premium. Medicaid work requirements (2027) not modeled: their income test counts all MAGI, so only income below about \$7,000 a year is affected. Above 400% FPL: full SLCSP (both modes). SLCSP user-supplied; FPL updated annually. Start year applies to both modes. Couple SLCSP scaling assumes user supplied the combined household premium; individual-only plans should use half the joint premium. |
| **Cash-flow balance** | Annual equality, in nominal dollars: net spending + surplus deposits + federal and state income taxes (NIIT included) + payroll tax + 10% early-withdrawal penalty + Medicare + ACA premiums + debt payments = wages + other inc + net inv + SS + pensions + SPIAs + big-ticket items + fixed-asset proceeds + withdrawals from all four account types. Contributions are not in it: they are deposited without leaving the cash flow (see Wages & contributions). Big-ticket items shift money into or out of specific years. | Single annual time step; no intra-year cash-flow detail. |
| **Asset allocation** | User-specified glide path (linear or s-curve) from initial to final allocation across four asset classes (S&P 500, corporate bonds, T-notes, cash). Per-account or per-individual or household-wide. | Accounts assumed rebalanced to target allocation. No optimization of allocation itself. |
| **Return rate models** | Constant: trailing_30, user, conservative, optimistic, historical_average. Varying: historical (replay), historical_gaussian, historical_lognormal, gaussian, lognormal, historical_bootstrap, vector_ar, garch_dcc, historical_copula (Gaussian copula — fits a 4×4 copula correlation matrix to the historical window; each asset's marginal distribution is preserved exactly via the empirical CDF with no parametric shape imposed; generates new year-combinations that honour all pairwise rank correlations), gmm (Gaussian Mixture Model — fits K multivariate Gaussian components on the historical window to capture regime-dependent cross-asset correlations; independent draws), hmm (Hidden Markov Model — extends GMM with a Markov transition matrix between K regimes fitted via Baum-Welch EM; adds temporal autocorrelation and regime persistence across years). Custom DataFrame model or pluggable Python modules via API. Optional reverse (time-reversal) and roll sequence transforms for varying methods. Optional `constrain_mean` flag (historical_gaussian, historical_lognormal, historical_copula, vector_ar, garch_dcc, gmm, hmm) shifts each generated series additively so its arithmetic mean matches the historical window mean, eliminating sampling bias without altering distribution shape. | Constant rates: no sequence-of-returns risk. Historical: plan horizon must fit in selected window. Stochastic methods require sufficient history for fitting. For parametric Gaussian-residual models (historical_gaussian, historical_lognormal, vector_ar, garch_dcc), a piecewise-linear (PWL) normalization transform is automatically applied to the inflation dimension before fitting to correct for the right-skewness of historical inflation; slopes are auto-calibrated from the selected date window and reported in the debug log. |
| **Simulation modes** | Historical back-test: replays past return sequences from selected window. Monte Carlo: stochastic sampling (historical_bootstrap, historical_gaussian, lognormal, etc.). Fixed-rate: constant or user-supplied returns. Stochastic spending optimization: solves a recourse LP across S scenarios to find a committed first-year spending level $g^*$ that maximizes spending subject to a target shortfall probability; sweeps risk-aversion parameter $\lambda$ to trace the efficient frontier (spending vs. expected shortfall). Optional longevity risk (MC-only) samples ages-at-death from selected mortality table by sex (`M`/`F`) and current age; each scenario is solved on its own sampled horizon (last-survivor for couples). | Historical: plan horizon must fit in data window; single deterministic path. Monte Carlo: sequence-of-returns risk; convergence depends on sample size. Historical-range augmented sampling uses reverse × roll combinations. Stochastic spending LP: non-anticipativity enforced only on first-year commitment; later years adapt to each scenario. Longevity+MC reproducibility requires separate seeding of both rate and longevity RNG streams. |
| **Retirement Efficiency Score (RES)** | Experimental Sharpe-ratio analog for historical scenarios: RES = (committed spending − floor) / CVaR, where CVaR is the floor-capped conditional value-at-risk of the shortfall distribution. The floor defaults to the HSF (historical spending floor). RES is maximized over the efficient frontier to find the optimal target success rate ρ\*. Available via `compute_res()` and `compute_cvar()` in the public API; shown as an experimental expander in the Spending Optimization UI (historical mode only). | Experimental; not validated for production use. MC RES is not exposed in the UI because the lognormal tail structure pushes ρ\* to artificially aggressive values (~30%) that do not reflect practical spending risk. |
| **Filing status** | MFJ until first spouse's death; then single. Standard deduction, brackets, SS thresholds, LTCG thresholds, and Medicare premiums transition automatically. | Head of household not modeled. Death year fixed at plan construction for the deterministic LP solve; stochastic mortality (sampled lifespans by sex and mortality table) is supported for Monte Carlo longevity-risk analysis (see Simulation modes). |
| **Transaction exclusions** | Two properties keep a plan readable: a Roth conversion and a tax-free withdrawal do not occur in the same year (above age 59½ — below it, a conversion ladder needs exactly that overlap), and a surplus is not banked in a year that also withdraws from a taxable or tax-free account. Both were once enforced with big-M binaries and are now restored after the solve: the conversion overlap by an exact substitution into a larger tax-deferred withdrawal, and the surplus round-trip by reporting the withdrawal net of the deposit returning to the same account. Neither changes balances, taxes, spending or the bequest. | Neither exclusion ever changed the optimum, so nothing is paid for them. Where the substitution provably does not hold — non-zero `oppCostX`, `withdrawalOrder="taxable_first"`, or one spouse converting while the other withdraws — the overlap is reported rather than removed. Only a round trip whose sale realizes no gain is netted: a sale at a gain is reported gross, as is the deposit that buys back, since the gain counts in the year's income, taxed or not, and the purchase resets the account's cost basis. |
| **Self-consistent (SC) loop** | Iteratively fixes nonlinear quantities (SS taxability, NIIT, LTCG bracket bounds, Medicare IRMAA, ACA premiums, OBBBA phaseout, and the taxable accounts' gain fractions when a cost basis is supplied) at previous values, solves LP, recomputes, repeats until convergence. Convergence requires both the objective and those fed-back quantities to settle: an iterate whose own income implies different premiums or a different taxable SS fraction is not a fixed point however still the objective looks. `residualTol` sets the bar, and every solved plan reports the remaining disagreement per tax family in `plan.fixedPointResidual`. | Convergence not guaranteed in edge cases. The loop can settle on a fixed point that is self-consistent yet not optimal, because the discontinuous quantities are constants within each LP and cannot be traded against income. Optional MIP modes (`withSSTaxability="optimize"`, `withLTCG="optimize"`, `withNIIT="optimize"`, `withACA="optimize"`, `withMedicare="optimize"`, `withSeniorBonus="optimize"`) carry them as binary variables at much higher cost. Two strategies solve them. Branch-and-bound (`mipStrategy="branch-and-bound"`, the default) is the solver's complete search: it stops at the solver gap, and it still sits inside the loop for the quantities that remain nonlinear (investment income, gain fractions, a conforming state's senior deduction), so its answer is not exact; it wants MOSEK: on a shipped case HiGHS ran 49–238× slower and proved optimality in none of the windows tried. Local search (`mipStrategy="local-search"`, or the preset `breakpointMethod="local-search"`) is fix-and-optimize around the loop's plan: much faster, never worse than the loop's plan, but not a proven optimum, and it can stop short of the optimum on the unrestricted problem. It does not cover `withdrawalOrder="taxable_first"`. With `withSSAges` set, it runs at each claiming month searched. |
| **Beneficiary fractions** | Fraction of each account type left to surviving spouse at first death. Default 1.0 for all (including HSA). The rest goes to non-spouse heirs as the partial bequest, reported after the heirs' tax on tax-deferred and HSA money. The objective counts it with a small weight (`partialBequestWeight`, default max(1%, twice the solver gap) of a dollar): without one, the optimizer is indifferent to money the household does not need, and the partial bequest is arbitrary. | The weight breaks ties rather than valuing the first heirs; the partial bequest can still differ by several percent between equally good plans. A larger weight is a real valuation, set by the user. |

### Access interfaces

Owl's modeling capabilities are available through four interfaces:

| Interface | How to use |
|-----------|------------|
| **Streamlit web UI** | Browser-based interactive tool — no installation. Hosted at [owlplanner.streamlit.app](https://owlplanner.streamlit.app) or self-hosted. |
| **Python API / Jupyter** | Import `owlplanner` directly; build and solve `Plan` objects programmatically. See `USER_GUIDE.md`. |
| **CLI (`owlcli`)** | Run, compare, and explain cases from the terminal using TOML case files. |
| **AI assistant (MCP)** | Connect Owl as a tool to Claude, Gemini, Cursor, Zed, VS Code (Copilot or Cline), or any MCP-compatible AI client. Describe your financial situation in plain language; the AI runs the optimizer and interprets the results. See `mcp.md`. |
