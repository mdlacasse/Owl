### Modeling Capabilities

This document summarizes all modeling components in Owl (Optimal wealth lab), how each is implemented, and the assumptions and limitations that apply. It serves as a quick reference for understanding the scope and boundaries of the optimizer.

| Component | Modeling approach | Assumptions & limitations |
|------------|--------------------|---------------------------|
| **Objectives** | maxSpending: maximize net spending subject to bequest floor. maxBequest: maximize after-tax bequest subject to spending floor; requires `netSpending` option to set the spending floor. Roth conversion amounts optimized in both (subject to caps and per-person controls). Optional `timePreference` (%/year) discounts future spending to reduce end-of-life back-loading. To see the trade-off between the two objectives rather than one point on it, sweep the `bequest` floor under maxSpending to trace the spending-vs-bequest trade-off curve. | LP objective is linear, so it always drives spending to an extreme of the feasible range; use profile slack and bequest/spending constraints to shape the solution. |
| **Spending profile** | User-specified shape (flat or smile) scaling a first-year basis. Survivorship factor at first death. Optimizer determines achievable basis. The profile is a bilateral constraint for both objectives: spending stays within ±slack% of the profile shape (`spendingSlack` sets the tolerance). | Profile shape fixed; only scale is optimized. |
| **Bequest objective** | After-tax estate value. Tax-deferred and HSA balances discounted by heirs' marginal rate. Surviving spouse inherits per beneficiary fractions; non-spouse portion taxed. | Single heirs' marginal tax rate applied to all inherited tax-deferred and HSA balances. |
| **Taxable accounts** | Dividend and interest yield proportional to balance each year; capital gains when optimizer elects to sell. All realized gains treated as long-term. Capital gains on withdrawals use the **average-cost** method: when a fraction of the account is sold, the same fraction of the total cost basis is recovered, so the realized gain equals `gain_fraction × withdrawal` where `gain_fraction = (balance − basis) / balance`. Optional: `setCostBasis([b1, b2])` (or TOML field `taxable_cost_basis`) supplies the current cost basis per person; the SC loop then tracks how basis evolves each year as shares are sold and new contributions (HFP contributions plus LP surplus deposits) add fresh basis, as do the dividends and interest taxed each year and reinvested in the account. Bond and cash returns are taxed yearly, so the account's unrealized gain is attributed to its equity share. Without a supplied basis, realized gains fall back to current-year price appreciation only (`(τ₀ − μ) × withdrawal`), which underestimates gains for accounts with substantial embedded appreciation. | All gains treated as long-term; short-term gains (holding period &lt; 1 year) not distinguished. Average-cost method only; FIFO and LIFO lot accounting not supported. Tax-loss harvesting not modeled. |
| **Start date (first year)** | Each plan year runs from January 1, and the first year is a full calendar year. Balances entered as of a later date are back-projected to January 1 for growth only, at the first year's assumed returns. Graphs and worksheets show the whole first year; the summary shows the net spending remaining in the year (first-year spending times the fraction of the year left). | Money already moved through the accounts this year is not added back, so with a start after January 1 the first year counts it again: spending paid so far (net of income received) is charged again, contributions already made are added again. Use January 1 balances when available; otherwise enter only the contributions, QCDs and big-ticket items still to come in the first year of the Wages and Contributions table, keeping wages (like SS and pensions) at full-year amounts, since that income offsets the spending already paid. Spending, an annual target, cannot be split this way; to offset it, add to the balances (most naturally the taxable account) the spending and taxes paid since January 1 minus the wages, benefits and pensions received since then. That net is positive for a retiree drawing down savings and usually negative for someone still saving. |
| **Safety net (taxable)** | Optional inflation-indexed minimum balance per individual's taxable account. Enforced from year 2 through each individual's life horizon. | First year excluded. Can cause infeasibility if minimum exceeds initial balance or desired bequest is smaller than survivor's safety net. |
| **Tax-deferred accounts** (401k, IRA, 403b) | Pre-tax contributions from Wages and Contributions table. Withdrawals taxed as ordinary income. RMDs enforced as minimum withdrawal floors from age 70–75 (birth-year dependent). 10% early withdrawal penalty before age 59½. | Pro-rata rule (IRS Form 8606) not modeled; entire balance treated as pre-tax. Early withdrawal exceptions (72(t), disability, etc.) not modeled. |
| **Roth accounts** | After-tax contributions. Five-year maturation rule enforced via minimum-balance constraints (recent conversions + contribution gains retained). Age 59½ threshold per individual. Conversion amounts optimized; taxable as ordinary income in year of conversion. Individual years can be held fixed instead of optimized by ticking the *Roth conv fixed* column of the Wages & Contributions table beside the amount in *Roth conv*: the conversion is then pinned to exactly that amount, bypassing the annual cap and the last-two-years restriction, and an amount of `0` pins the year at no conversion. | Contribution principal not separately tracked; some valid early withdrawals of principal conservatively disallowed. Pro-rata rule not modeled. Roth conversions disallowed in last two years of plan horizon (unless that year is held fixed). |
| **Health Savings Accounts (HSA)** | Fourth account type. Pre-tax contributions reduce ordinary income, provisional income, and MAGI. Contributions zeroed at Medicare enrollment age (IRC §223: Medicare disqualifies contributions). Withdrawals capped at total qualified medical expenses (QMEs): Medicare costs plus `setMedicalExpenses()` amount (dental, vision, co-pays, etc.). Pre-Medicare years: only the `setMedicalExpenses()` amount is eligible. Spouse inherits intact. | IRS contribution limits not enforced; user responsible. HDHP eligibility assumed, not verified. Catch-up (\$1,000 for 55+) not auto-applied. Mid-year Medicare: entire year zeroed (IRS allows proration). Pre-65 non-qualified penalty (20%) not modeled for withdrawals exceeding QMEs. |
| **Fixed assets** | Real estate, annuities, collectibles, etc. An asset is listed from the first year it is present in January, so one bought during 2026 carries `year` 2027, and one sold during its `yod` is still listed that year with the proceeds reaching savings the following January. Disposition generates proceeds split into ordinary income, LTCG, and tax-free portions by asset type. Growth rate from acquisition to disposition year. Sales commission applied. **Type-specific tax treatment:** `residence` — IRC §121 primary residence exclusion applied ($250k single / $500k MFJ); gain above the exclusion is LTCG, excluded gain and basis are tax-free. `fixed annuity` — gain above basis is ordinary income; basis returned tax-free. All other types (`real estate`, `stocks`, `collectibles`, `precious metals`) — full gain is LTCG, basis is tax-free. | Fixed outflows; disposition timing and amount user-specified. No optimization of sale timing. |
| **Debts** | Amortized payments from start year for term years. Principal and interest from standard amortization. Enters cash-flow as fixed outflows. | No optimization of paydown vs. invest. Mortgage interest not deducted (standard deduction assumed). |
| **Wages & contributions** | Year-indexed table: wages, other income, net inv, contributions to all account types, Roth conversions, QCDs, big-ticket items. Five-year lookback for Roth maturation. Only the `year` column is required; any other column may be omitted and is read as zero. | User-supplied; no optimization of contribution amounts. HSA contributions zeroed at Medicare age. |
| **Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs)** | Per-person, per-year amounts from the `QCD` column of the Wages & Contributions table. Excluded from AGI, so they lower federal and state taxable income, Social Security taxability, and the MAGI feeding IRMAA and NIIT. Credited against the RMD dollar-for-dollar, drawn from the tax-deferred balance, and never available as spending. Age 70.5 and the annual per-person limit ($108k in 2025, $115k in 2026, indexed; later years projected at a fixed 3%) are enforced as errors. Reported as a `Charitable giving` slice in the lifetime-allocation and cash-flow-mix charts, as a dedicated *Charitable Giving (QCD)* chart splitting each gift at the RMD it satisfies, as its own worksheet column, and in the case description. | Amount is user-specified, not optimized. IRA-only eligibility not checked: Owl aggregates IRA with 401(k)/403(b) into one tax-deferred account. A 401(k) balance must be rolled over to an IRA first to be eligible, which is a real-world step but a non-event in the model. Anti-abuse offset for deductible IRA contributions after 70.5 not modeled. One-time split-interest-entity QCD (CGA/CRT) not modeled. |
| **Social Security benefits** | PIA × age-based adjustment factor. FRA by birth year (65–67). Own, spousal, and survivor benefits computed from PIA and DOB. Benefits are prorated in the year they start, by the claiming month (same as pension). Taxability (0–85%) from provisional income formula converged via the SC loop. Optional trim for trust-fund scenarios. Claiming ages are user-specified by default. Optional: `withSSTaxability="optimize"` replaces the SC-loop taxability approximation with an exact MILP formulation. Optional: `withSSAges="optimize"` co-optimizes SS claiming ages (monthly resolution, 62–70) via MILP. **Survivor benefits:** the survivor's own benefit and the survivor benefit are tracked as separate streams and the larger is paid each year, so a survivor who has not yet claimed keeps their own (still growing) benefit. Amount is set by the deceased's record — the greater of their benefit at death and 82.5% of their PIA (CFR §404.338); a worker who died before claiming leaves the full PIA plus the DRCs earned up to death — and fixed at death (no posthumous DRCs). The reduction is measured against the survivor's own age at filing, linearly from 71.5% at 60 to 100% at the survivor FRA (a distinct schedule from the retirement FRA, up to 4 months earlier); no credit accrues past it. `social_security_survivor_claim_age` sets the timing: `"immediate"` (default), `"fra"`, or an explicit age. | Family maximum benefit cap not modeled. Earnings test not modeled; users assumed fully retired before FRA. Survivor claiming date is user-specified, not optimized. WEP and GPO repealed (2025); not modeled. |
| **Pension income** | Monthly amount and commencement age per individual. Optional inflation indexing. First-year proration by fraction of year remaining. Joint-and-survivor fraction for survivor benefit. | Early-commencement reductions and late-commencement credits not modeled. |
| **SPIA (Single Premium Immediate Annuity)** | IRA/401k rollover funds a SPIA: premium deducted from tax-deferred account in buy year (non-taxable event); annual payments begin in the same year as purchase as fully taxable ordinary income. Optional CPI indexing. Optional joint-and-survivor fraction for couples. Payments increase MAGI, affecting Medicare IRMAA surcharges and Social Security taxability. Multiple SPIAs supported per plan. | Premium assumed drawn entirely from tax-deferred account (IRA rollover); non-IRA funding not modeled. Partial-year proration of income in buy year not modeled (full annual payment assumed). Annuity pricing (premium-to-income ratio) is user-supplied; no built-in pricing model. |
| **Ordinary income tax** | Seven brackets (OBBBA 2026: 10–37%). Standard deduction + 65+ additional + OBBBA §1002 bonus (phases out by MAGI). Bracket widths and rates inflation-adjusted. Optional reversion to pre-TCJA rates. | Itemized deductions not modeled; standard deduction only. OBBBA 65+ bonus phaseout computed outside LP (post-solution update). |
| **State income tax** | All 50 states + DC supported via `state` field in `[basic_info]`. Graduated brackets; brackets, deduction and exemption caps grow with inflation only where the state indexes them (`brackets_indexed`, `deduction_indexed`, `exemptions_indexed`; NY and NJ stay nominal). Filing status transitions MFJ→Single at spouse death. State standard deduction (or the federal one, age 65+ additions included, for states that allow it), plus per-filer personal and 65+ exemptions where the state has them (e.g. NJ, MD, OH). Personal credits (AR, CA, DE, IA, NE, OR, and CA's 65+ credit) reduce the state tax down to zero. State tax base is federal AGI. NY benefit recapture (above \$107,650 NY AGI) is charged through the self-consistent loop: its amount is paid but the LP does not see its marginal effect. Optional `locality` adds a city tax on top (its brackets are extra continuous LP variables). Optional `moves` change the state of residence (and locality) at given years: the residence on December 31 taxes the whole year, so the year of a move is taxed by the new state; the optimizer times conversions and withdrawals around it. Upstream Owl takes one move; this fork takes several. Optional retirement income exemption (age-gated dollar cap, e.g. NY \$20k, CO \$24k age 65+). Optional pension exemption (e.g. KY \$31,110, IL fully exempt). NJ pension/retirement and other retirement income exclusions (lines 28a-28c): the share of eligible income excluded steps down at \$100k/\$125k/\$150k of NJ income, as cliffs, modeled exactly with one binary per tier per year in which a filer is 62+. Optional SS exclusion (e.g. FL/TX zero SS tax; KS SS exempt below \$75k AGI — binary flag, not phased out). Capital gains taxed as ordinary income at state level. State LP variables are continuous except the NJ exclusion's tier binaries (three per eligible year). | State AMT and other state-specific credits not modeled; income phase-outs of exemptions and credits not modeled. Local taxes: only those in `taxes_local.toml` (NYC brackets on NY taxable income, Yonkers surcharge on NY tax); their household and school credits are not modeled. SS threshold exemption (KS \$75k, MO \$100k) treated as binary (always taxed) — conservative for high-income plans. Retirement income exemption applies to IRA withdrawals only; SPIA and annuity income treated as ordinary. NJ: the Special Exclusion (no Social Security) and disability before 62 are not modeled; when only one spouse is 62+, only line 28a is taken; NJ basis in IRAs (non-deductible NJ contributions) is not modeled. NJ tier binaries are free only in years within 1.5x the top ceiling of the previous iteration's income; without `maxTime`, MILPs carrying them stop after 20,000 HiGHS nodes (MOSEK: 60 s), keeping that MILP's tiers and reporting its gap (large balances can end a few tenths of a percent from proven optimal). Bracket rates in `taxes_state.toml` require annual review. |
| **Long-term capital gains tax** | Three brackets (0%, 15%, 20%) based on total taxable income. LTCG stacks on ordinary income. By default, bracket thresholds are determined via the SC loop (prior-year ordinary income fixes bracket bounds; optimizer fills lowest-rate brackets first). Optional: `withLTCG="optimize"` replaces the SC-loop approximation with a MILP formulation, solved by branch-and-bound or local search (`mipStrategy`), using binary variables (`zl`) to select the correct 0%/15%/20% bracket each year. | All realized gains treated as long-term; short-term gains not modeled. |
| **Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)** | 3.8% on lesser of NII and MAGI excess over \$200k (single) / \$250k (MFJ). NII = dividends, interest, LTCG, rent/trust from `net inv` column. Converged via self-consistent loop. Optional: `withNIIT="optimize"` uses a MILP formulation with one binary variable per year (`zj`) selecting which term of the minimum bounds the tax; most effective when `withLTCG="optimize"` is also set. | Thresholds not indexed for inflation (per IRS). |
| **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** | IRS Uniform Lifetime Table III for standard cases; IRS Joint and Last Survivor Table II (Pub 590-B) when spouse is the sole beneficiary and more than 10 years younger. Starting age: 70 (pre-1949), 72 (1949–1950), 73 (1951–1959), 75 (1960+). Fractions applied to prior-year-end balance. | Inherited IRA and beneficiary RMD rules not modeled. |
| **Medicare Part B & Part D** | Part B and Part D premiums (base + IRMAA surcharges) from MAGI two years prior. Part D uses same brackets as Part B; optional base premium (default 0) and optional disable for other drug coverage. By default, IRMAA surcharges are determined via the SC loop (prior MAGI fixes bracket selection each iteration). Optional: `withMedicare="optimize"` replaces the SC-loop approximation with a MILP formulation for bracket selection; can be disabled to reduce MIP size and improve solve time when IRMAA precision is not needed. | IRMAA thresholds assumed to scale with general inflation; CMS may use different index. First two plan years require user-supplied prior MAGI (default zero). Part D base is user-configurable; default 0. |
| **ACA marketplace (pre-65)** | Net Premium Tax Credit (PTC) cost: user sets SLCSP benchmark premium; PTC computed from MAGI and FPL. For couples, the SLCSP should be the combined household premium; when the older spouse transitions to Medicare, the SLCSP is automatically scaled to the remaining spouse's individual plan using the CMS age rating curve (45 CFR 147.102), prorating by age-rating factors at the transition year. Optional start year defers ACA coverage (years before start treated as employer-covered, zero ACA cost). Two modes: **loop** (default, `withACA` unset or `"loop"`) uses SC iteration with year-aware acaCosts; **optimize** (`withACA="optimize"`) uses LP/MIP to co-optimize MAGI vs ACA cost. Up to 138% FPL the household is assumed covered by Medicaid at no premium, in both modes (the rule in expansion states). | **Loop**: Year-aware (2025/2026 rules). Exact sliding scale. ACA fixed per MAGI each iteration. **Optimize**: 2026 rules only; no year-awareness. Sliding scale priced by tangent lines (a few dollars below the statute at most). LP can reduce ACA by shifting MAGI, including down to the 138% Medicaid line. Non-expansion states (no Medicaid for most adults, marketplace rules from 100% FPL) not modeled. Above 400% FPL: full SLCSP (both modes). SLCSP user-supplied; FPL updated annually. Start year applies to both modes. Couple SLCSP scaling assumes user supplied the combined household premium; individual-only plans should use half the joint premium. |
| **Cash-flow balance** | Annual equality: spending + taxes + Medicare + debt payments + contributions = wages + SS + pension + withdrawals + fixed-asset proceeds + other income. All amounts inflation-adjusted (today's dollars). Big-ticket items shift spending into specific years. | Single annual time step; no intra-year cash-flow detail. |
| **Asset allocation** | User-specified glide path (linear or s-curve) from initial to final allocation across four asset classes (S&P 500, corporate bonds, T-notes, cash). Per-account or per-individual or household-wide. | Accounts assumed rebalanced to target allocation. No optimization of allocation itself. |
| **Return rate models** | Constant: trailing_30, user, conservative, optimistic, historical_average. Varying: historical (replay), historical_gaussian, historical_lognormal, gaussian, lognormal, historical_bootstrap, vector_ar, garch_dcc, historical_copula (Gaussian copula — fits a 4×4 copula correlation matrix to the historical window; each asset's marginal distribution is preserved exactly via the empirical CDF with no parametric shape imposed; generates new year-combinations that honour all pairwise rank correlations), gmm (Gaussian Mixture Model — fits K multivariate Gaussian components on the historical window to capture regime-dependent cross-asset correlations; independent draws), hmm (Hidden Markov Model — extends GMM with a Markov transition matrix between K regimes fitted via Baum-Welch EM; adds temporal autocorrelation and regime persistence across years). Custom DataFrame model or pluggable Python modules via API. Optional reverse (time-reversal) and roll sequence transforms for varying methods. Optional `constrain_mean` flag (histogaussian, histolognormal, historical_copula, garch_dcc, gmm, hmm) shifts each generated series additively so its arithmetic mean matches the historical window mean, eliminating sampling bias without altering distribution shape. | Constant rates: no sequence-of-returns risk. Historical: plan horizon must fit in selected window. Stochastic methods require sufficient history for fitting. For parametric Gaussian-residual models (historical_gaussian, historical_lognormal, vector_ar, garch_dcc), a piecewise-linear (PWL) normalization transform is automatically applied to the inflation dimension before fitting to correct for the right-skewness of historical inflation; slopes are auto-calibrated from the selected date window and reported in the debug log. |
| **Simulation modes** | Historical back-test: replays past return sequences from selected window. Monte Carlo: stochastic sampling (historical_bootstrap, historical_gaussian, lognormal, etc.). Fixed-rate: constant or user-supplied returns. Stochastic spending optimization: solves a recourse LP across S scenarios to find a committed first-year spending level $g^*$ that maximizes spending subject to a target shortfall probability; sweeps risk-aversion parameter $\lambda$ to trace the efficient frontier (spending vs. expected shortfall). Optional longevity risk (MC-only) samples ages-at-death from selected mortality table by sex (`M`/`F`) and current age; each scenario is solved on its own sampled horizon (last-survivor for couples). | Historical: plan horizon must fit in data window; single deterministic path. Monte Carlo: sequence-of-returns risk; convergence depends on sample size. Historical-range augmented sampling uses reverse × roll combinations. Stochastic spending LP: non-anticipativity enforced only on first-year commitment; later years adapt to each scenario. Longevity+MC reproducibility requires separate seeding of both rate and longevity RNG streams. |
| **Retirement Efficiency Score (RES)** | Experimental Sharpe-ratio analog for historical scenarios: RES = (committed spending − floor) / CVaR, where CVaR is the floor-capped conditional value-at-risk of the shortfall distribution. The floor defaults to the HSF (historical spending floor). RES is maximized over the efficient frontier to find the optimal target success rate ρ\*. Available via `compute_res()` and `compute_cvar()` in the public API; shown as an experimental expander in the Spending Optimization UI (historical mode only). | Experimental; not validated for production use. MC RES is not exposed in the UI because the lognormal tail structure pushes ρ\* to artificially aggressive values (~30%) that do not reflect practical spending risk. |
| **Filing status** | MFJ until first spouse's death; then single. Standard deduction, brackets, SS thresholds, LTCG thresholds, and Medicare premiums transition automatically. | Head of household not modeled. Death year fixed at plan construction for the deterministic LP solve; stochastic mortality (sampled lifespans by sex and mortality table) is supported for Monte Carlo longevity-risk analysis (see Simulation modes). |
| **Transaction exclusions** | Two properties keep a plan readable: a Roth conversion and a tax-free withdrawal do not occur in the same year (above age 59½ — below it, a conversion ladder needs exactly that overlap), and a surplus is not banked in a year that also withdraws from a taxable or tax-free account. Both were once enforced with big-M binaries and are now restored after the solve: the conversion overlap by an exact substitution into a larger tax-deferred withdrawal, and the surplus round-trip by reporting the withdrawal net of the deposit returning to the same account. Neither changes balances, taxes, spending or the bequest. | Neither exclusion ever changed the optimum, so nothing is paid for them. Where the substitution provably does not hold — non-zero `oppCostX`, `withdrawalOrder="taxable_first"`, or one spouse converting while the other withdraws — the overlap is reported rather than removed. Years whose gains reach a taxed bracket are reported gross so the withdrawal always accounts for the tax beside it. |
| **Self-consistent (SC) loop** | Iteratively fixes nonlinear quantities (SS taxability, NIIT, LTCG bracket bounds, Medicare IRMAA, OBBBA phaseout) at previous values, solves LP, recomputes, repeats until convergence. Convergence requires both the objective and those fed-back quantities to settle: an iterate whose own income implies different premiums or a different taxable SS fraction is not a fixed point however still the objective looks. `residualTol` sets the bar, and every solved plan reports the remaining disagreement per tax family in `plan.fixedPointResidual`. | Convergence not guaranteed in edge cases. The loop can settle on a fixed point that is self-consistent yet not optimal, because the discontinuous quantities are constants within each LP and cannot be traded against income. Optional MIP modes (`withSSTaxability="optimize"`, `withLTCG="optimize"`, `withNIIT="optimize"`, `withACA="optimize"`, `withMedicare="optimize"`) carry them as binary variables at much higher cost. Two strategies solve them. Branch-and-bound (`mipStrategy="branch-and-bound"`, the default) is the solver's complete search: it stops at the solver gap, and it still sits inside the loop for the quantities that remain nonlinear (investment income, gain fractions, the OBBBA phaseout), so its answer is not exact; it wants MOSEK: on a shipped case HiGHS ran 49–238× slower and proved optimality in none of the windows tried. Local search (`mipStrategy="local-search"`, or the preset `breakpointMethod="local-search"`) is fix-and-optimize around the loop's plan: much faster, never worse than the loop's plan, but not a proven optimum, and it can stop short of the optimum on the unrestricted problem. It does not cover `withSSAges="optimize"` or `withdrawalOrder="taxable_first"`. |
| **Beneficiary fractions** | Fraction of each account type left to surviving spouse at first death. Default 1.0 for all (including HSA). The rest goes to non-spouse heirs as the partial bequest, reported after the heirs' tax on tax-deferred and HSA money. The objective counts it with a small weight (`partialBequestWeight`, default 0.001 of a dollar): without one, the optimizer is indifferent to money the household does not need, and the partial bequest is arbitrary. | The weight breaks ties rather than valuing the first heirs; the partial bequest can still differ by several percent between equally good plans. A larger weight is a real valuation, set by the user. |

### Access interfaces

Owl's modeling capabilities are available through four interfaces:

| Interface | How to use |
|-----------|------------|
| **Streamlit web UI** | Browser-based interactive tool — no installation. Hosted at [owlplanner.streamlit.app](https://owlplanner.streamlit.app) or self-hosted. |
| **Python API / Jupyter** | Import `owlplanner` directly; build and solve `Plan` objects programmatically. See `USER_GUIDE.md`. |
| **CLI (`owlcli`)** | Run, compare, and explain cases from the terminal using TOML case files. |
| **AI assistant (MCP)** | Connect Owl as a tool to Claude, Gemini, Cursor, Zed, VS Code (Copilot or Cline), or any MCP-compatible AI client. Describe your financial situation in plain language; the AI runs the optimizer and interprets the results. See `mcp.md`. |
